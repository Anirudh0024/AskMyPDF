CORRELATION VS COVARIANCE — STATISTICS NOTES
==============================================

1. COVARIANCE
------------
Definition:
Covariance measures how two variables vary together. It indicates whether they tend to move in the same direction or in opposite directions.

Formula (population covariance):
Cov(X, Y) = Σ[(Xi - μX)(Yi - μY)] / N

Formula (sample covariance):
sXY = Σ[(Xi - X̄)(Yi - Ȳ)] / (n - 1)

Interpretation:
- Positive covariance: X and Y tend to increase or decrease together.
- Negative covariance: when X is above its mean, Y tends to be below its mean, and vice versa.
- Covariance near zero: there is little or no linear co-movement in the data. This does not rule out a nonlinear relationship.

Important point:
- Covariance depends on the units and scales of the variables.
- Its numerical size is difficult to interpret by itself.

2. CORRELATION
--------------
Definition:
Correlation measures the strength and direction of the LINEAR relationship between two variables. Pearson's correlation coefficient is the most commonly used form.

Formula:
r = Cov(X, Y) / (sX × sY)

where:
- Cov(X, Y) = sample covariance
- sX = sample standard deviation of X
- sY = sample standard deviation of Y

Interpretation of Pearson's r:
- r = +1: perfect positive linear relationship.
- r close to +1: strong positive linear relationship.
- r = 0: no linear relationship (a nonlinear relationship may still exist).
- r close to -1: strong negative linear relationship.
- r = -1: perfect negative linear relationship.

Important points:
- Correlation is unitless.
- Its value always lies between -1 and +1.
- It is easier to compare across datasets than covariance.

3. KEY DIFFERENCES
------------------
Covariance:
- Shows the direction of joint variation.
- Can take any real value.
- Depends on the units/scales of the variables.
- Magnitude is not standardized, so it is hard to compare.

Correlation:
- Shows direction and strength of a linear relationship.
- Always lies between -1 and +1.
- Has no units.
- Standardized, so comparisons are easier.

4. SIMPLE EXAMPLE
-----------------
Suppose study time and exam score tend to increase together.

- Their covariance would generally be positive.
- Their correlation would also be positive if the relationship is linear.
- A correlation of +0.85 would indicate a strong positive linear relationship.
- The covariance's numerical value depends on how study time and scores are measured.

5. RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN THEM
----------------------------
Correlation is standardized covariance:

Correlation = Covariance / (standard deviation of X × standard deviation of Y)

Therefore:
- If both standard deviations are positive, covariance and Pearson correlation have the same sign.
- Correlation rescales covariance to a fixed range from -1 to +1.
- If either variable has zero variance (it is constant), Pearson correlation is undefined.

6. COMMON MISUNDERSTANDINGS
---------------------------
- Correlation does not prove causation.
- A correlation of zero means no linear relationship, not necessarily no relationship at all.
- A large covariance does not automatically mean a strong relationship because its size depends on units.
- Pearson correlation can be affected by outliers.
- Pearson correlation describes linear association; other methods may be needed for nonlinear patterns.

7. QUICK MEMORY TRICK
---------------------
COVARIANCE = direction of how two variables vary together.
CORRELATION = direction + strength of their linear relationship, on a standardized scale.
